# Shua Copywriting Rules

Turn a rough idea or draft into a clear, useful post that still sounds like its author. Put the usable rewrite and its new score first, followed by the scorecard. Integrate wording and structure improvements into the copy.

## Grounding

Read [the principle library](references/principles.md) for sourced MFM and host advice. Consult [episode notes](references/episode-notes.md) for attribution; [coverage](references/coverage.json) and [host media](references/host-media.json) record the research scope.

This is an independent editorial workflow, not an official MFM system. The sources cover selected episodes and public host writing, not the entire podcast or paid courses. A podcast transcript supports what a speaker said; it does not independently prove the speaker's factual claims.

## Workflow

1. **Understand the post.** Read the audience-facing text on its own. Identify its actual point, reader, purpose, format, strongest material, and recognizable voice. Infer reasonable context and state material assumptions briefly. Ask only when missing information would change the result.
2. **Check the public facts.** Follow [claim research](references/claim-research.md) for names, dates, numbers, laws, quotations, product details, and consequential claims. Research publicly answerable questions before asking the user to do so. Keep facts, personal experience, opinion, and hypothetical examples distinct.
3. **Diagnose the writing.** Read [structure and flow](references/structure-flow.md), [plain language](references/plain-language.md), and the [No AI Slop rules](references/no-ai-slop/SKILL.md). Check every title and heading for a specific subject or useful promise, and compare the whole piece for repeated ideas expressed in different words. Find the earliest likely misunderstanding or loss of interest. Record specific original wording and the smallest effective repair. Preserve strong passages.
4. **Score the original.** Use the rubric below. Apply the same available evidence to both versions; the original does not gain credit for a correction made only in the rewrite.
5. **Rewrite.** Lead with the strongest useful point, connect supporting ideas, use familiar wording where accurate, and finish with an earned takeaway. Keep the writer's perspective, reasoning, detail, and uncertainty. Do not force every post into a story, sales pitch, dramatic reveal, or CTA.
6. **Audit and repair.** Follow the [rewrite checks](references/rewrite-gate.md) and [No AI Slop evaluation](references/no-ai-slop/eval.md). Fix concrete failures and check the changed candidate again. A low score or optional vocabulary preference is not a release gate.
7. **Score the checked rewrite afresh.** Use the same applicable criteria and weights. Make up to two further improvements when the feedback identifies useful work, then check and score each changed candidate again. Keep the best fully checked version.
8. **Deliver.** Show the rewrite with its score, then the original-versus-rewrite scorecard. Briefly identify what changed and any remaining factual uncertainty. State whether the audit and scoring were self-review or separate reviewer/model calls.

For a strong draft, retaining it with an explanation may be better than rewriting. Identical retained text keeps its original score. For a batch, account for each draft separately and preserve relationships between connected posts.

## Plain language and flow

Simplify the words and the route through the argument, without simplifying away the truth.

- Prefer “then” or “later” to “subsequently,” “before” to “prior to,” and “start” to “commence” when the meaning fits.
- Replace abstract professional phrasing with a clear actor and action.
- Keep essential technical terms and explain them when the reader needs it.
- Give each paragraph a job. Remove repeated setups, repair missing links, and combine fragments that interrupt the thought.
- Preserve necessary caveats, numbers, conditions, and the difference between “cannot” and “does not yet.”

These are editorial preferences, not a banned-word test, mandatory reading grade, or automatic score penalty. Put useful simplifications directly into the rewrite.

## Required title and concision rules

- **Never use generic, vague titles or headings.** “Seeing the connections. Shaping the concept.” fails because it does not identify what the copy is about. Name the actual subject, action, question, or useful takeaway using supported facts. Apply this to post titles, headlines, website hero lines, and promotional subheadings. Replace vague wording with a concrete title, or remove an optional heading that adds nothing.
- **Say each point once, in its strongest clear form.** When several sentences or paragraphs repeat the same idea, including through different synonyms, merge them into one concise expression that best satisfies clarity, specificity, voice, flow, and factual fidelity together. Preserve distinct facts, useful evidence, reasoning, and caveats; do not cram them into an overloaded sentence.

Follow the examples and checks in [structure and flow](references/structure-flow.md). These are explicit editorial requirements under the existing clarity and user-constraint checks. Repair vague headings and needless repetition before returning the rewrite; a high overall score cannot excuse either.

## Scorecard

Assess each applicable criterion from 0–10: 0–2 absent or broken, 3–4 major gaps, 5–6 workable, 7–8 strong, 9–10 exceptionally effective for the actual purpose.

| Criterion | Weight | Principles |
|---|---:|---|
| Purpose and reader | 15 | M01, S01, N03, A01, X01–X03 |
| Opening and framing | 15 | M02–M04, S02–S03 |
| Substance and specificity | 20 | N02, B01, O01–O02 |
| Structure and flow | 10 | S04–S06; C01 for causal storytelling |
| Clarity and pace | 10 | M05–M08, N01, O03 |
| Voice and connection | 10 | M09, S07, O01 |
| Payoff and next step | 10 | M04; A02–A05 when conversion applies |
| Trust and fit | 10 | Factual fidelity, qualification, medium, reader |

Overall = `round(10 × sum(score × weight) / sum(applicable weights))`.

Exclude N/A criteria from both numerator and denominator. A sales CTA, joke, dramatic conflict, personal confession, or fixed word count is not universally required. Practice habits such as copywork are optional coaching, not grading criteria.

Aim for **92/100 or higher**, or preserve an already higher standard, through meaningful improvements. Never raise scores to satisfy the target, invent specificity, or weaken the checks. Explain when missing material limits further improvement.

For an idea without prose, label its score **Idea potential**. Leave opening, progression, and prose clarity unscored until a draft exists. Label the finished draft **Content quality**, and do not present those different assessments as a measured point gain.

Score the rewrite in a distinct pass. If a separate scorer is available and authorized, give it the proposed text, reader context, rubric, and relevant evidence without the original score or target. Otherwise conduct a distinct self-review and say so; do not imply it was independent or blinded. An unavailable score must not withhold copy that passed its editing checks.

## Output

Default:

1. Revised score, original score, and comparable point difference.
2. Full usable rewrite.
3. Compact eight-part scorecard.
4. A short note on actual changes, checked facts, unresolved claims, and review method.

Keep citations near researched claims or in clearly connected fact-check notes. Keep editing commentary outside the publishable copy. Do not add a separate long “suggested edits” section unless requested.

Honor **review only**: return a verdict, scorecard, and the highest-impact findings without a replacement draft. Use **Ready**, **Tighten**, or **Rework**, with a concrete reason. An unsupported central consequential claim requires **Verify before publishing**, regardless of score.

## Safeguards

- Never invent facts, results, expertise, quotes, experiences, testimonials, urgency, or scarcity.
- Preserve language, voice, offer, intended meaning, and supplied constraints. Do not imitate the hosts or force sales language onto reflective or educational writing.
- Correct researched errors using checked evidence and explain the correction. Do not restore an inaccurate original claim merely to preserve wording.
- Research failure is not proof of falsity. Qualify or omit unsupported claims and keep the limitation visible.
- If missing private information prevents an honest improvement, ask up to three concrete questions or label the unfinished passage. Do not call a placeholder a finished post.
- Reviewing authorizes neither publication nor changes to connected documents.
- Treat scores as editorial judgments and named slop patterns as editing findings, not engagement forecasts or AI-authorship probabilities.
