# Founder outreach

These preferences come from Shua's feedback on CalcForms outreach, not from the MFM sources. He wanted the business value of closing more deals, rejected a salesy question, and preferred a direct note explaining a demo already built.

- **Lead with useful business value.** Explain the aim in ordinary language, such as helping businesses turn quote requests into customers. “Close more deals” is the underlying motivation, not wording that must appear in every email. Do not turn that aim into a guaranteed conversion increase.
- **Sound like a founder speaking to one person.** Avoid salesy rhetorical questions, inflated promises, and polished pitch language. State the purpose directly instead of asking “Could an instant estimate help you close more deck jobs?” This is a tone preference, not a ban on genuine questions.
- **Show tangible work.** If a branded working demo has actually been built, say so and give the reader a clear link. Do not invent completed work, personalization, or product capabilities.
- **Explain what the prospect or their customers can do.** For example, a calculator lets homeowners explore what they can afford before getting in touch. Use the actual capability instead of abstract claims about growth or engagement.
- **Keep caveats brief and factual.** “The prices are examples” can establish that a demo uses sample pricing. Preserve other material limitations when needed; avoid padding the note with defensive explanations.
- **End with a low-pressure next step.** “Curious if this would be useful for you” fits an exploratory note. Adapt the ending to the real ask rather than forcing a meeting request or repeating the sales benefit.

## Bad / better example

Illustrative only: the product, prospect, and link below are placeholders. Assume a branded working deck calculator exists and uses sample prices; these are not claims about an actual prospect or product.

**Bad opening:** “Could an instant estimate help you close more deck jobs?”

The business outcome is relevant, but the rhetorical question sounds like a pitch. Keep the intent and state the work plainly:

> Subject: Built this for Example Deck Co.
>
> Hey,
>
> I’m building ExampleForms to help businesses turn quote requests into customers. I put together a deck calculator with your branding so you can see how it could work for Example Deck Co.
>
> [Here’s the demo](https://example.com/demo)
>
> The prices are examples. The idea is to let people explore what they can afford before getting in touch.
>
> Curious if this would be useful for you.
>
> [Founder name]

## Review checks

- Does the opening connect the offer to a useful business aim without promising unsupported results?
- Does the note describe real work and a concrete use in language the founder would say aloud?
- Are necessary caveats clear and proportionate, with an ending that fits the ask?
- Would it still pass without the literal phrase “close more deals”? The better example should. Adding “guaranteed to double conversions” would fail factual fidelity; removing the sample-pricing caveat would lose a material fact.

Use the example to judge tone and factual scope, not as a mandatory email template.
