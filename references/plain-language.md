# Plain-language suggestions

Adapted from [Plain Language by Arjun Prabhulal](https://github.com/arjunprabhulal/agent-skills/blob/5223b745ca356a04a42250ee316cd8723775f43f/skills/docs/plain-language/SKILL.md), under the [MIT license](plain-language/LICENSE). The [pinned original](plain-language/upstream.md) is provenance, not an additional workflow or gate; this adaptation governs its use here.

Work these preferences into the existing rewrite and line-edit suggestions:

- Infer the reader's knowledge and purpose from context. Prefer familiar wording when it means the same thing: “before” for “prior to,” or “check” for “conduct an examination of.” These are contextual examples, not forbidden words.
- Keep proper names, quotes, numbers, and precise terms readers need. Briefly explain an essential unfamiliar term only when the supplied material supports the explanation. If a simpler equivalent would lose meaning, keep the original.
- Turn unnecessary abstract nouns into verbs, cut empty phrases and untangle crowded sentences where helpful. Preserve personal perspective, distinctive phrasing, useful depth and cadence. Brevity is not the goal by itself.
- Make a deliberate wording pass in the same edit: replace abstract professional phrasing with a familiar equivalent where accurate, spell out vague noun phrases, and keep the actor and action easy to find. For example, “commercial viability remains to be demonstrated” can become “we still need to see whether it works as a business.” Treat this as a contextual suggestion, not a fixed substitution.
- Make a deliberate structure pass: put the strongest relevant point early, group related sentences, and connect the concrete example to its consequence and takeaway. Give each paragraph a clear job. Merge isolated fragments when they interrupt the thought, split overloaded passages where helpful, and remove repeated setups and conclusions. Keep the original's distinct insight; do not cut the lesson while removing a stock introduction. Adapt to the platform and voice without imposing a fixed outline or paragraph length.
- Preserve scope, conditions, uncertainty, consequential caveats, and distinctions such as “cannot” versus “does not yet.” Never invent a definition, analogy, actor, example or attribution to make a passage easier to read.
- Put useful changes directly in the rewrite and suggested replacements. In the existing explanation of changes, briefly name the actual wording and structure improvements. Do not claim either was improved when it was deliberately retained. No separate vocabulary report is required.

This is advisory only. Do not add a gate, banned-word list, required reading grade, word-frequency threshold, sentence-length limit, automatic score deduction or extra review pass. A word's rarity alone is not a content weakness. Optional synonyms belong in suggestions, not mandatory fixes. Do not ask extra questions solely to simplify vocabulary.

The existing meaning, factual-fidelity, voice and No AI Slop checks still apply. A rewrite may retain uncommon words and pass. A simpler rewrite that changes a fact or erases uncertainty still needs repair under those existing checks.

## Familiar-word pass

Prefer “then” or “after that” to “subsequently,” “so” to “consequently,” “before” to “prior to,” “start” to “commence,” and “show” to “demonstrate” where the meaning permits. Inspect these explicitly while rewriting. Keep exact quotes, names and essential technical terms. These are contextual preferences, not banned words, score penalties or release gates. Also check paragraph order and sentence connections using structure-flow.md.
