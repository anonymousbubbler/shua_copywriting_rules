# Sourced principle library

Concise paraphrases; episode IDs resolve in [episode-notes.md](episode-notes.md). Conditional techniques are tools, not universal requirements. Application notes are our editorial interpretation.

## M — Writing masterclass (E01)

- **M01:** Decide the desired feeling, action, and single remembered takeaway.
- **M02:** Lead with the strongest relevant point; remove throat-clearing.
- **M03:** Test whether the opening makes a stranger want the next sentence.
- **M04:** Create curiosity, then deliver the promised answer promptly.
- **M05:** Explain the idea aloud before polishing its wording.
- **M06:** Prefer familiar vocabulary and accessible language.
- **M07:** Favor short sentences; vary their length for rhythm.
- **M08:** Edit out filler and move valuable material forward.
- **M09:** Preserve your actual interests and voice.
- **M10:** Use a headline and one-sentence summary to clarify direction.

Practice: warm up with copywork; draft quickly; take a break; edit deliberately. Keep a swipe file of effective openings, endings, and phrases. Compare spoken and written versions with a voice memo.

## S — Storytelling masterclass (E02)

- **S01:** Match pace and context to the medium and existing audience trust.
- **S02:** Frame an idea around why it matters to this reader.
- **S03:** Offer an interesting thought, not just attractive packaging.
- **S04:** Give a story a character's intention and an obstacle.
- **S05:** Show what emotionally matters; ordinary situations can carry stakes.
- **S06:** Find the specific moment of change and shape the story around it.
- **S07:** Address one person conversationally, anticipating their questions.
- **S08:** Humor can support a serious point; surprise and contrast can create it.

Application: do not demand a dramatic transformation in every post. The episode discusses storytelling, including frameworks credited to Matthew Dicks and examples from comedy.

## N — Neville Medhora interview (E03)

- **N01:** Excessive formality and large words can obstruct communication.
- **N02:** A demonstration, image, or brief video may explain more efficiently than prose.
- **N03:** Understand the intended person's motivations; select the communication format accordingly.

Application: assess a caption and demo together when both are available. Do not penalize a caption for information supplied visually. If the asset is unseen, identify that limitation.

## A — Persuasive sequence (E05)

- **A01:** Start from the audience's motivations.
- **A02:** Attention: create a reason to notice.
- **A03:** Interest: provide relevant, compelling information.
- **A04:** Desire: connect the offer to a benefit the reader values.
- **A05:** Action: make the intended next step explicit and usable.

Sam discusses AIDA. Apply it to persuasive or conversion-oriented posts without imposing four labeled sections. His water-and-muscle example is explicitly invented; never treat it as factual health advice or supporting evidence.

## B — Product framing (E06)

- **B01:** Explain the valuable experience or outcome beyond the product category.

Application: distinguish a meaningful benefit from decorative adjectives. The episode's sales anecdotes illustrate framing; they do not verify performance or justify misleading embellishment.

## C — Causal storytelling (E09)

- **C01:** Build progression through obstacles and consequences, not merely successive events.

Shaan credits the South Park creators for the contrast between “and then” and “but/therefore.” Application: diagnose causality, not the presence of particular words. Tutorials and chronological records may legitimately use sequences.

## O — Originality and sustained interest (E08, E13)

- **O01:** Let genuine curiosity and particular expertise shape what you publish. (E08, E13)
- **O02:** Improve the underlying material before obsessing over presentation. (E13)
- **O03:** Diagnose boredom rather than treating length alone as the problem. (E13)
- **O04:** Improve one deliberate element with each new piece. (E13)

Application: respect actual platform and user length constraints. Do not repeat success rhetoric as a guarantee. These are content-development heuristics, not a validated growth formula.

## H — Advertising and humor (E12)

- **H01:** Sell the customer's improved experience, not merely the object.
- **H02:** Establish persuasive structure before adding comedy.
- **H03:** Keep jokes that advance understanding of the product or message.
- **H04:** Earn continued attention incrementally.

Application: the guest describes an advertising-team process. Do not require a comedy team, retreat, or commercial formula for ordinary posts.

## P — Practice and calibration (E04, E07, E10, E11)

- **P01:** Judge a piece against the response it was meant to produce. (E04)
- **P02:** Collect memorable expressions for inspiration. (E07) Adapt rather than copying distinctive passages; this safeguard is ours.
- **P03:** Find authentic stories by recalling the first, latest, best, and worst experience in a category. (E10)
- **P04:** Separate newsletter-business execution from writing quality. E11 mostly concerns building a media company; no universal post check was extracted.

Practice habits do not prove a finished draft is weak. Do not grade writers on whether they hand-copied examples or took a prescribed break.


## X — Public host writing and interviews

- **X01:** Decide which readers matter and which feeling to deliver. Distinctive interests and experience give people a reason to follow. (shaan-audience-mailbag)
- **X02:** Research the conversations your audience already has, then contribute a useful, distinctive angle. Do not invent evidence that this research happened. (sam-capitalism-transcript, 18:50–23:25)
- **X03:** Connect the discovery context, headline, story, and intended emotion. (sam-capitalism-transcript, 14:11–17:21)
- **X04:** A chosen constraint can stimulate creativity. Assess supplied word/format constraints; do not demand universal brevity. (shaan-creative-constraints)
- **X05:** For product copy, explain the customer's improved situation, not just ingredients or the seller. (shaan-big-mario)
- **X06:** Optional practice: hand-copy admired work to notice patterns and develop your own voice. (sam-copythat-public-method)
- **X07:** Compare an original attempt with a revision and the feedback that motivated it. (shaan-power-writing-public-page)

Public course descriptions are the evidence here; no paid lessons were accessed. Practice is coaching, never a scoring requirement.
