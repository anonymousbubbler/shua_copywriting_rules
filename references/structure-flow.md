# Structure and flow

Use these checks for the actual form of the post. They adapt the general structure and flow review used with this skill and the clarity, voice, paragraph-flow, and proof passes in [Corey Haines' copy-editing skill](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/tree/e85f3cbc2e4a79bf33b2a12e9bdd9087d414735b/skills/copy-editing). The [MIT notice](copy-editing/LICENSE) and [provenance](copy-editing/provenance.json) accompany this adaptation. These procedures are not attributed to the MFM hosts.

## Read before repairing

Read the copy without using the writer's explanation or source packet to fill gaps. State what literally happens or what claim the post makes, using its actual wording. Name any missing actor, definition, reason, or connection.

Classify the form: argument, explainer, reflection, story, list, announcement, instruction, or a hybrid. A post about a transaction can be an argument about its business implications; it need not become a dramatic story.

## Specific titles

Never write or retain a generic, vague title, headline, website hero line, or promotional subheading. This is a project-specific requirement, not a quotation or rule attributed to the MFM hosts.

- **Read it without the body.** The heading should identify the actual subject, action, question, or useful content the reader will get. Typography, a logo, or an explanatory paragraph cannot rescue an empty slogan.
- **Check whether it says anything specific.** “Seeing the connections. Shaping the concept.” and “Unlocking new possibilities” could describe countless unrelated businesses. They fail even if they sound polished.
- **Repair from the material.** Use the actual work, project, mechanism, problem, or takeaway. “How ADU condo sales work” identifies a subject; “Planning residential projects from concept to launch” identifies work. These are illustrative options, suitable only when supported by the supplied copy.
- **Do not manufacture specificity.** Never add an unsupported number, outcome, credential, or claim to make a heading more concrete. Replace a vague title with a supported factual label, or remove it when optional. Ask for missing substance only when it is essential.
- **Keep useful labels and precise terms.** Navigation labels such as “Contact” and “Pricing” are functional labels. A phrase such as “Utility connections for ADU conversions” is specific; the word “connections” itself is not banned. A post need not have a separate title.

Apply this check to every heading and any proposed alternative, including headings split across lines. Record the exact wording and why it passes or needs repair.

## One clear version of each point

Keep the copy concise by removing repetition of meaning, not merely repeated words.

1. Identify the claim or contribution of each sentence and paragraph. Compare the title, opening, body, and ending for the same point restated with different phrasing.
2. Group overlapping statements. Keep or compose the version that best preserves clarity, specificity, voice, logical flow, and factual accuracy together. No score target overrides those requirements.
3. Merge useful details into that version and delete the redundant formulations. If the details need more than one sentence, keep a short, connected passage rather than forcing everything into one crowded sentence.
4. Preserve new evidence, an explanation of how something works, a necessary caveat, or a distinct consequence. Those add information; they are not duplicates merely because they concern the same topic.
5. Read the whole result again. The opening should establish the point, the body should develop it, and the ending should deliver a useful takeaway or limit instead of saying the same thing a third time. A heading and its supporting explanation can share a topic; each must do a different job.

Illustrative edit: “We manage the conversion process. We coordinate each step of the conversion. We oversee the conversion from start to finish.” becomes “We manage the conversion from start to finish.”

If another sentence adds “Lender approval is still required,” keep that condition. Repetition of a precise term for clarity is also useful; do not create synonym cycling just to avoid repeating a word. Recaps belong only where the format genuinely needs them and they help the reader.

## Check the progression

1. **Promise:** Identify what the opening leads the reader to expect. Apply the specific-title check to every heading. Locate the passage that fulfills the promise. Narrow an overstated promise rather than increasing the ending's certainty.
2. **Paragraph jobs:** Reverse-outline what each paragraph adds. Apply the whole-piece repetition check above; combine duplicate ideas even when the wording differs. Keep useful context and intentional changes in pace.
3. **Order:** Explain each adjacent relationship: evidence, cause, consequence, example, qualification, or another item in a list. A new conjunction cannot fix a missing logical connection.
4. **Sentence handoffs:** Check pronouns, actors, chronology, terminology, and scope. Readers should not need to reread to find what “this” refers to.
5. **Concrete meaning:** Find the fact, mechanism, example, or reasoning supporting each essential abstraction. Keep personal judgments visibly distinct from established facts.
6. **Rhythm:** Read the copy continuously. Rejoin staged fragments and vary repetitive sentence shapes where helpful. Do not impose a universal sentence-length limit.
7. **Voice:** Preserve the draft's perspective, level of formality, uncertainty, and useful character. Without a voice sample, judge clarity and genericness rather than claiming a verified voice match.
8. **Ending:** Finish on a specific takeaway, meaningful limit, or relevant next step. Remove repeated lessons and generic declarations that the topic is “worth watching.”

For a real story, check the supplied situation, goal, obstacle, action, outcome, and changed understanding. Do not invent those elements. For an argument, connect the claim, evidence, limits, and implication. For a list, distinct useful items can be enough.

## Record and recheck

For each substantive weakness, record the exact span, reader effect, proposed repair, and result after revision. Use Pass, Needs work, Unverified, or N/A. Distinguish missing evidence from unclear prose.

Integrate these findings into the existing structure, clarity, payoff, and meaning criteria. They are not an additional release gate. After simplifying vocabulary, read the entire post again to ensure the argument still connects and the original insight remains.
